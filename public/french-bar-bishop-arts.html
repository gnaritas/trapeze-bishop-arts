The bar · Bishop Arts

A bar that never crosses the Atlantic

The wine list is entirely French — full stop. Around it sits everything France distills, ferments, and pours: cognac, absinthe, Chartreuse, rhum agricole from the French Caribbean, cider, beer, and the rituals of the aperitif and the digestif.

The deep end

Cognac, put first

An extensive cognac list is a statement in any American city — it says the bar expects you to slow down. Two Deep Ellum bar veterans built this program, and it shows: the spirit France brandies gets the shelf space, poured neat, ordered by house if you want a tour.

The green end

Absinthe & Chartreuse, in the old lines

Cocktails are built on classic French and New Orleans-esque lines with French spirits and liqueurs at the center — absinthe and Chartreuse doing the work they did in the cabaret bars this room is modeled on.

The islands

Rhum agricole

France’s Caribbean departments distill rum from pressed cane juice, not molasses — grassier, peppery, more itself. The bar carries those rhums agricoles, the bottles the Atlantic French world actually drinks.

The ceremonies

Aperitif before, digestif after

A solid menu of aperitifs and digestifs — the drink that opens the evening and the one that closes it, treated as real courses of a night rather than add-ons. Come early; the first hour is its own reason to come.

Without the ceremony

Wine, cider, or a beer — fine

Every bottle of wine is French; the cider and beer are French and “French-adjacent.” You can do the whole cognac pilgrimage or just order a glass of something cold. The bar is the living half of the cabaret idea — and performers get announced on Instagram, the same place the hours and the bill for any given night get posted.
