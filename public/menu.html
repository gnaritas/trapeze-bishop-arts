The board

Short list. Long night.

The kitchen opened with a handful of old-school French plates and has been widening it since — the bar, meanwhile, never leaves France. What follows is the board as CultureMap reported it at opening. Tonight’s printed menu is the live answer, and it lives at the table.

From the kitchen

The plates named at opening

  • French onion soup — onions cooked down, good broth, a browned cap of cheese. The barometer of any French brasserie.
  • Oysters with leeks — hot oysters paired with leeks, the Marais-style move on the half shell.
  • Steak au poivre — seared steak under crushed peppercorns, cream pan sauce poured over.
  • A burger — sitting on the list among the classics, the way Paris menus have always done.
  • Duck confit poutine — fries, cheese curds, gravy, and the duck on top: Quebec’s late-night snack, rethought by a French kitchen.

The kitchen, in detail

From the bar

France, from the first pour

  • All-French wine — every bottle on the list is from France. The list doesn’t cross the border.
  • Cognac — an extensive list of France’s brandy, poured neat.
  • Absinthe & Chartreuse — cocktails built on the old French lines, with a New Orleans vein running through them.
  • Rhum agricole — rums from the French Caribbean, distilled from pressed cane juice, not molasses.
  • Aperitifs & digestifs — the before and the after — both taken seriously here.
  • Cider & beer — French and “French-adjacent,” for when a bottle of something big is too much ceremony.
