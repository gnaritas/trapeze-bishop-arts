Trapèze

Bien mangé, bien bu — good food, good drink

A French bohemian bar and bistro in a rebuilt Oak Cliff bungalow, at the west end of Bishop Arts. Old-school French plates. An all-French wine list. Cognac, absinthe, and music going late — a place built for losing track of time.

The idea

An old-fashioned bohemian artist’s enclave

Trapèze isn’t a themed room — it’s modeled on the old French bohemian bars and cabarets, where the art was the evening. The owners wanted to make one in the neighborhood that already has French roots: Bishop Arts was home to La Réunion, the French utopian colony settled here in the 1850s, and the district still throws a Bastille Day party.

“We’re trying to do a tribute to the French history of Oak Cliff and Bishop Arts,” Novotny told CultureMap, “and our goal is to capture the things we love about regional French cuisine and culture.”

The same team runs Ruins and Armoury D.E. in Deep Ellum.

The house

Kept its old face. New bones.

The bungalow on West 8th sits in a conservation district, so the rebuild had to honor the street it stands on — the exterior was rebuilt entirely, the original look preserved. Out front, a hand-worked sign by the sculptural sign-makers at Decotron reads Bien Mangé, Bien Bu: good food, good drink. Inside, the plan is colorful, a little quirky, and built for long nights. There’s a patio, too.

The kitchen

Old-school French, cooked by a New York résumé

Chef Jeremy Spector — Balthazar, Gramercy Tavern, his own New York kitchens — directs the menu. It runs through the regions of France, with flavors pulled from France’s colonial empire, Morocco included. The opening list was short and confident: French onion soup, oysters with leeks, steak au poivre, a burger, duck confit poutine.

The bar

A bar that never crosses the Atlantic

Every wine on the list is French. Around it: an extensive cognac list, absinthe and Chartreuse cocktails in the classic French and New Orleans lines, French-Caribbean rhum agricoles, aperitifs and digestifs, and French — and French-adjacent — cider and beer.
