The kitchen · Bishop Arts

Dinner, the old French way

Not bistro as a costume — bistro as a set of convictions: good broth, real butter, pepper put in by hand, and a kitchen that has cooked in the rooms that made those rules. Chef Jeremy Spector runs it.

French onion soup

The barometer · onions, broth, a browned cheese cap

Every city has a barometer of whether its French kitchen takes itself seriously, and it’s served in a crock. Onions cooked down until sweet, real broth, a lid of browned cheese that takes a spoon to break. The opening list starts here, which says what the kitchen is for.

Oysters with leeks · steak au poivre

The brasserie double header

Hot oysters partnered with leeks is the Marais order — shellfish treated like a course, not a platter. And steak au poivre is the plate a French kitchen is judged on: seared steak under a crust of crushed peppercorns, cream pan sauce finished in the pan, pepper put in by hand so the crust has texture. A burger rides along with them, French-menus style.

Duck confit poutine

French technique · Quebec’s great snack

Fries, cheese curds, gravy — the Montreal late-night plate — with duck confit on top instead of whatever came to hand. It’s the plate that tells you how this kitchen thinks: technique from France, appetite from anywhere the night goes.

Behind the pass

The chef, and the wider map

The owners brought in Jeremy Spector — New York-bred, Balthazar, Gramercy Tavern, his own New York restaurants — to direct the whole program. Per CultureMap, the menu represents the regions of France, with flavors from France’s once-vast colonial empire, Moroccan cooking among them. The five plates named at opening are the short version; the full list — brunch included — was promised right behind them.

What’s actually live tonight moves with the kitchen. The floor and Instagram are the live answer; prices live at the table, never on this site.
